# About the Robotics World for Marine Energy

The ocean energy industry is facing a growing challenge associated with the lack of trained personnel to complete deployment, operations, maintenance, and decommissioning (DOMD) of WECs and TECs. There is not currently a path to enter the workforce in marine energy DOMD, so there is a significant opportunity to advance workforce development efforts by including robotics education. The existence of this workforce will be very important to the viability of grid, community and distributed scale marine energy deployments. The goal of this research was to simulate ocean energy environments in the Gazebo physics simulator and create modules in ROS2 to provide a tool and curriculum for community colleges, trade schools, and marine energy deployers to train job seekers in operating of remotely operated vehicles (ROV) for DOMD. 
This project was able to successfully create a robotics world in which marine energy devices could be situated to enable students and professionals to simulate robotic systems in the waters around a marine energy device. This research leveraged existing robotic simulation environments that are the state of the art in ocean robotics to provide a very realistic opportunity for students to learn robotics and their connection to marine energy. Another key output of this research was identifying the existing workforce development opportunities in the space of marine robotics and marine energy. Also, because robotics are used extensively in other marine energy industries, a survey of existing marine robotics was completed to inform the marine energy industry.
